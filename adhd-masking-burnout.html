What ADHD masking looked like for me

I looked fine on the outside for years. On the inside I was barely coping. This is what that was like, how it turned into burnout, and what finally helped.

From the outside, I looked fine.

I showed up. I smiled. I ran the house and kept the kids fed and dressed. I did "fine" so well that some days even I believed it.

On the inside I was grieving and overwhelmed. I felt like I was coming apart, and nobody knew.

That's what masking is. The outside looks calm while the inside works flat out to hold it all together.

A lot of ADHD folks do this without knowing it has a name. I did it for most of my life.

It started long before I knew I had ADHD

As a kid I was described as calm and shy. That's what people saw.

What they didn't see was the panic underneath. My inside tempo was always fast. I had to pump myself up just to say hello to someone new. I got very good at hiding how I felt.

I always thought there was something wrong with me. That I couldn't do life like everyone else.

When the mask started to crack

In 2016 I was four months pregnant with my first child when my mum died suddenly. On top of the grief, I had postpartum anxiety that nobody picked up.

I was learning to keep a tiny human alive while I could barely keep myself together. I put so much pressure on myself to do it all well. Even breastfeeding was hard, and then I pressured myself about that too.

My second child came in 2019. Then 2020 and lockdown took away the small things that kept me steady, like seeing friends. The days were long. I started having dread and panic attacks about how I would get through them.

I tried anxiety medication. It just made me zone out, so I stopped.

The old school reports

We moved in with my dad for a while. Going through my old school reports, things started to click. So did watching Dad leave every cupboard open... the exact thing my partner complains about with me.

My trouble focusing on one thing at a time. The pattern that ran through my whole life.

At 37 I finally got an ADHD diagnosis. Everything about my past made sense. I felt seen for the first time.

But I still felt overwhelmed.

How masking turned into burnout

Masking takes so much energy. You live your life, and you also do the extra job of looking like you're coping.

For a long time I kept going anyway. Then one day I couldn't keep it up. The mask cracked and the burnout hit. I finally had to face what my nervous system had been telling me all along.

My body was stuck in fight and flight. Being told to rest more didn't touch it.

What I tried, and what finally helped

I tried a lot of things over the years. Therapy helped a bit, and EMDR was the most useful. I tried natural therapies. Medication made it easier to cope for a while, but it also made me numb, and sometimes I'd snap at the kids.

Around the same time as my diagnosis, I found breathwork. I physically felt different after my very first session. And I kept feeling it long after the session ended.

For the first time I knew in my body what calm could feel like. At peace. No threat. Like I was steering my own life instead of scrambling to survive it.

That's when I understood what I needed. Support for my nervous system, so my body could come out of fight and flight. If you want to know how a session works, I've written about what breathwork is and why it suits ADHD folks.

What changed, a few months in

Over a few months, the anxiety in my body eased.

I started to get a gap between something happening and how I reacted. Things that used to make me snap had no charge anymore.

I took my kids on a plane on my own. I got through conflict with patience.

I still have ADHD. My body just stopped fighting itself, so I felt capable.

I also got clear on what I wanted, for the first time. What mattered to me, and where I wanted my family and my life to go. I'd been too busy surviving to even think about it.

What I wish someone had told me

That the way I felt wasn't normal, but it also wasn't uncommon. That I needed support. That there wasn't anything wrong with me, and it wasn't my fault.

That it was so hard, and so much work, to keep living and masking the way I had my whole life.

And that it was safe now to stop. 💛

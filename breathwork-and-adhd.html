Breathwork and ADHD

Why stress affects an ADHD system differently, why meditation often does not work, and what guided breathwork does instead.

Most advice about managing stress assumes a brain that can sit still, hold its attention where it is placed, and follow a routine without external structure. If you have ADHD, you will have noticed that this advice tends not to survive contact with your actual life.

This page is about why that happens, and where breathwork fits.

Why stress affects ADHD differently

Under stress, the first capacities to reduce are working memory and cognitive flexibility. That is your ability to hold several things in mind at once, move between them, consider a different approach, and pause before responding.

With ADHD, those capacities are already under pressure. Stress therefore removes capacity from the area where there is least to spare.

This is why a demanding period can look like ADHD symptoms worsening significantly, and why the same person can manage well in one month and be unable to answer a simple email in another. The underlying condition has not changed. The available capacity has.

The additional load

Masking requires continuous effort, and the cost is usually not visible until the energy has already gone.

Emotional responses often arrive more quickly and with more intensity, which means more activation across an ordinary day.

Attention driven by interest can override signals such as hunger, thirst and fatigue for hours, followed by a sharp drop.

And for people who have spent years being told they are too much, too sensitive or too disorganised, the body may have learned that being visible carries risk. That is not a belief that can be argued with. It is a physical response that occurs before thought.

Why rest often does not work

Rest depends on your system registering that stopping is safe. If it has not, lying down does not produce rest. It produces a period of lying down while remaining activated.

This is why people describe getting into bed and their thinking speeding up, or taking a week off and becoming unwell on the second day. The activation did not stop when the demands did.

Why meditation often does not work with ADHD

Most meditation instructions ask you to sit still, sustain attention on one object, and return your attention each time it moves.

For some people that is workable. For an ADHD brain it asks for sustained self directed attention, which is the specific area of difficulty, without external structure and without feedback about whether it is being done correctly.

When it does not go well, the common conclusion is that regulation practices do not work for you. A more accurate conclusion is that the instruction was poorly matched to how your attention operates.

What guided breathwork does differently

There is a rhythm to follow, which gives your attention an anchor rather than an instruction.

The body has an active task, which is considerably easier than being asked to do nothing.

Guidance continues throughout, so there is no point at which you are left alone to work out whether you are doing it correctly.

And the session has a defined start and end, which removes the need to decide when to stop or to sustain the practice through your own effort.

For many of the people I work with, that combination is the entire difference between a practice they abandon and one they can actually use.

What the evidence does and does not show

Research into slow and controlled breathing has found changes in measures such as heart rate variability, along with reductions in stress and anxiety in a number of studies.

There is very little research on breathwork specifically for ADHD. Any claim that breathwork treats ADHD goes further than the current evidence supports, and further than I am willing to go.

Breathwork is not a substitute for ADHD assessment, medication or therapy where those are what you need. It sits alongside them. What it addresses is the state your system is in, which affects how much of your capacity is available to you on a given day.

What people report

These are not guaranteed outcomes, and not from a single session.

  • Recovering more quickly after something goes wrong
  • Noticing activation earlier, while there is still something you can do about it
  • Being able to rest without needing to earn it first
  • Less physical bracing in ordinary situations
  • Staying in a difficult conversation rather than shutting down or escalating
  • Having a practice that does not depend on remembering to do it alone

Is it safe?

Breathwork is not appropriate for every person or every health condition, and the way it is guided matters.

If you are pregnant, or you have a heart condition, epilepsy, high blood pressure, glaucoma or a psychiatric diagnosis, contact me before booking so we can determine whether it is appropriate, and consult your doctor if you are uncertain.

This is a wellbeing and educational practice. It is not medical treatment, psychotherapy or crisis support.
